Already faster than the pull? It turns you toward the hook instead of adding speed (gravity on a swing still can). Hooking from the floor pops you up. Near the hook the pull stops and you coast in. Swing, pump with WASD, let go to fly on with all that speed, jump to let go with a kick. Reeling into your own hook point is never a wall slam. 45 m range, 0.6 s cooldown, any surface |
| Wall-run, wall-jump | working | Hold forward along a wall in the air. Low gravity for 1.6 s, camera leans. Jump to kick off. Same wall can't be reused until you land |
| Vault, ledge climb | working | Run into anything 0.45 to 1.25 m high and you vault it, keeping speed. Jump at a ledge up to 2.3 m and you climb up |
| Dive and roll | working | Q (or Alt): lunge toward where you look (+4 m/s, low hitbox), from the ground or once per jump, 1 s cooldown. Landing from a dive rolls you forward keeping your speed; hold crouch to roll into a slide |
| Belly slide | working | Keep holding dive as you land and you slide along on your front instead of rolling (+1 m/s on impact, a little less friction than a slide, weak steering, speeds up downhill). Lowest hitbox in the game (0.8 m) and you can shoot the whole way. Let go (or jump) to get up; let go while holding crouch to go straight into a slide. If the slide runs out while you are still holding dive you crawl (2 m/s), except on a slope steeper than about 10 degrees, where you keep sliding down it. Under a gap too low to crouch in you stay down until you crawl out |
| Glide | working | Hold Shift in mid-air (much like Warframe's Aim Glide): your up-and-down speed is cancelled and you carry on the way you were already going, free to look and aim anywhere. Your speed bleeds off toward a 4 m/s drift, and you sink: hardly at all at first, up to 2 m/s by the end. From a standstill you just hang in place. You get 1.5 s of glide per time in the air and can spend it in pieces (let go to stop, press again to go on); landing, a wall run or the grapple refills it, and GLIDE on the HUD shows what is left. It takes a press in the air, so jumping with sprint held does not start one, and not within 0.5 m of the floor, so Shift as you land is still just sprint. **Guns scatter half as much while you glide** (the crosshair tightens to show it). Any other move takes over from it (flip, dive, grapple, wall run, wall jump, ledge), so it also works as a way to stop rising or falling before the next move. **Rocket boots while gliding** do not end it: the burst sends the whole glide off the way you press (WASD as you face; forward with nothing held), with all the speed you had plus the burst's. A hard shove knocks you out of it; a bullet only nudges you along. The legs trail behind the way you are going and the body tips into it, while the top half keeps aiming; a soft wind plays while you glide; everyone sees and hears it. Because it slows you, it is for hanging in the air and shooting, not for crossing long gaps fast. Arena bots do not use it yet (race bots do, at glide gaps). It replaced the float (straight-line, full-speed, with spinning legs that hit people). Numbers in movement_tuning.gd (Glide), GLIDE_SPREAD in player.gd and at the top of robot_animator.gd |
| Flips (air flip dodge) | working | Press jump again in mid-air: flip toward your WASD direction with a small burst, once per jump (refreshed by wall runs and the grapple). A jump pressed just above the floor still counts as a bunny-hop |

## Known issues

- Animations have only been judged from rendered still frames so far, not in motion on a
  real PC. Timing and feel will need a playtest pass.
- The muzzle flashes are still simple placeholders.
- The sound effects have only been checked by measurement and automated tests, not
  listened to on a real PC yet: expect to tune levels in `game/audio/sfx_list.gd`.
  That goes double for Halcyon's low gravity chord and terminal hum, which are meant
  to set a mood.
- Cirrus has only been run in a software renderer, automated tests and short headless
  bot games, never on a real graphics card or with people. Its frame rate, how a fall
  and a ride feel, whether 8 s is enough warning from deep inside a building, and its
  sounds (above all how loud the tick is) need checking on a PC. Bots getting off
  doomed islands was only seen at one change.
- Halcyon has only been run in a software renderer and in headless bot matches, never
  on a real graphics card: its frame rate, and how the blue event lighting reads in a
  real fight, need checking on a PC.
- The sniper rifle has no scope or zoom: right mouse is the grapple. It can be added
  with a new key if wanted.
- No LAN address detection on some networks: the lobby's code may stand for 127.0.0.1 (the
  lobby shows the address too). Use `ipconfig` to find your address and join with that if so.
- Lobby codes hold an IPv4 address only; a host reachable only over IPv6 needs the address typed.
